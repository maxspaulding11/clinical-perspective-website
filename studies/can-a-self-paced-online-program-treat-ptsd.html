Can a self-paced online program treat PTSD from childhood trauma?

Covered September 13, 2026

By Maxamus Spaulding, BA

181 adults with PTSD linked to childhood interpersonal trauma tried an online program combining self-guided modules with optional virtual group sessions. It beat standard care on symptom reduction, with benefits still holding four months later.

Adults whose PTSD traces back to childhood abuse or other interpersonal trauma often struggle to access specialized treatment, especially outside major cities. This trial tested "Trauma PORTAL," a hybrid program combining eight self-paced online psychoeducation and skills modules with eight optional one-hour virtual group sessions led by trauma therapists. Researchers in Ontario, Canada randomly assigned 181 adults (81% women, average age 41) with PTSD from childhood interpersonal trauma to either Trauma PORTAL or standard care. After eight weeks, the program group's PTSD symptoms (measured on the PCL-5 scale) had dropped about 7 points more than the standard-care group, a moderate effect, and that gap held steady at the 16-week follow-up. No adverse events were reported. The main limitation is that this was a single site with an assessor-blind but not participant-blind design, and it compared the program to routine care rather than to an existing gold-standard trauma therapy, so it shows the approach works better than doing nothing in particular, not that it matches specialized in-person treatment.
