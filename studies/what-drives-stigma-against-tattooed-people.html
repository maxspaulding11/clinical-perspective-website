Stigma

What drives stigma against tattooed people?

Covered July 22, 2026

An Ecuadorian survey used factor analysis to unpack how tattoo stigma operates, especially within the justice system.

Researchers in Ecuador surveyed adults to understand what drives social stigma against tattooed people, particularly within the justice system context. Using a digital questionnaire and statistical factor analysis, they found a clear pattern of negative stereotyping toward tattooed individuals. Interestingly, having tattoos yourself or knowing tattooed people closely was linked to less prejudice, while age, gender, and occupation made no real difference. The takeaway: tattoos still act as a visual cue that triggers snap moral judgments, even in settings like courtrooms where impartiality is supposed to rule, suggesting a real need for training that reduces appearance-based bias in legal and forensic decision-making.

The study

Héctor Ramiro Carvajal-Romero, Martha Cecilia Carvajal-Blandín, Bernardo Peña-Herrera, Andrés Ricardo Delgado-Reyes, Multidisciplinary Latin American Journal (MLAJ), Vol. 4, No. 2, May 2026

DOI: 10.62131/MLAJ-V4-N2-001
