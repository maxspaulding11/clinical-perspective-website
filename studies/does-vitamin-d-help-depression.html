Myth Check

Does vitamin D help depression?

Covered August 10, 2026

108 young people with both major depression and low vitamin D were given supplements. The effect was real but modest.

Vitamin D gets talked about as a natural mood fix, so this trial is a useful, careful reality check. Researchers in Taiwan studied 108 young people (ages 10–24) who had both major depression and low vitamin D, giving them either 4,800 IU of vitamin D3 daily for 8 weeks or a placebo. The headline result is nuanced: overall, vitamin D did NOT beat placebo for depression itself. It did modestly reduce anxiety in the randomized vitamin D group. And when researchers zoomed in on participants with moderate-to-severe depression, that subgroup showed reductions in both depression and anxiety versus placebo, though the adolescent slice of that subgroup didn’t clearly benefit. No safety problems (like high calcium) appeared. So what does it mean? For a young person who is genuinely vitamin-D deficient and more severely depressed, supplementation might help as one piece of care. But this is a small, exploratory study, and it does not show that vitamin D lifts depression for everyone. It’s a promising lead worth bigger trials, not a standalone treatment, and definitely not a reason to skip established care. Helpful for some, not a cure.

The study

Yi-Ting Lee, et al., European Journal of Nutrition, July 2026

DOI: 10.1007/s00394-026-04061-0
