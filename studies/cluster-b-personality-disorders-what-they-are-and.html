Cluster B personality disorders: what they are and what actually helps

Covered July 14, 2026

A review of borderline, histrionic, and narcissistic PD — their origins, the therapies with real evidence, and why culture shapes both.

This review looks at three Cluster B personality disorders—borderline, histrionic, and narcissistic—and explains what they are, why they develop, and which treatments help. These disorders affect how people manage emotions, relate to others, and understand themselves. Research shows they often arise from a mix of genetics, early life stress, and differences in how the brain handles emotions. Therapies like Dialectical Behavior Therapy, Schema Therapy, and Mentalization‑Based Therapy can make a real difference, especially for borderline personality disorder, which carries a high risk of self‑harm. The review also highlights that culture matters. In places like Kenya, stigma and community expectations can make symptoms worse or delay treatment. Because of this, mental‑health care needs to be adapted to fit local cultural realities. Overall, the findings support more flexible diagnostic tools, better‑trained clinicians, and treatment approaches that consider both biology and culture.

The study

Patricia Wanjiru Wanderi, et al., Journal of Sociology, Psychology and Religious, April 2026
