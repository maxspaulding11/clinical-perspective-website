The real link between childhood maltreatment and narcissism

Covered August 19, 2026

Across 2,157 people, early harm didn't simply 'create narcissists' — it shaped which narcissistic traits emerged.

“Narcissists are made by their childhoods” is a popular claim, but this study shows the real link is more specific and more interesting than the meme. Researchers drew on five cohorts totaling 2,157 people, including healthy volunteers and a large clinical sample (mostly people with major depression, plus schizophrenia-spectrum, bipolar, and stress-related conditions). They measured childhood maltreatment alongside two distinct faces of narcissism: “admiration” (the grandiose, self-promoting, look-how-great-I-am side) and “rivalry” (the defensive, antagonistic, protect-my-status-by-putting-others-down side). Childhood maltreatment was linked to lower admiration and higher rivalry, small effects, but consistent. In plain terms, early harm wasn’t associated with more confident grandiosity; if anything it tracked with less of it, and with more of the brittle, defensive kind of narcissism. Emotional abuse, emotional neglect, and physical neglect were the maltreatment types most consistently tied to this pattern. The associations differed somewhat between healthy and clinical groups, and because the data are cross-sectional, this shows correlation, not proof of cause. Still, it reframes a common story: adversity doesn’t simply create narcissists. It may shape which narcissistic traits emerge, nudging people toward the guarded, rivalrous style rather than the openly grandiose one.

The study

Janette Ratzsch, et al., Child Abuse & Neglect, July 2026

DOI: 10.1016/j.chiabu.2026.108227
