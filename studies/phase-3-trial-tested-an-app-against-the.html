Psychosis

A phase 3 trial tested an app against the symptoms of schizophrenia no drug is approved for

Covered October 22, 2026

By Maxamus Spaulding, BA

No medication is approved for the negative symptoms of schizophrenia. In a 457-person double-blind trial, a smartphone therapeutic beat a sham app on motivation and pleasure, by a modest margin.

Negative symptoms of schizophrenia - blunted motivation, reduced pleasure, social withdrawal - drive much of the long-term disability, and no drug is approved in the US to treat them. Psychosocial interventions help, but access and sustained engagement limit them. Researchers led by Abhishek Pratap ran CONVOKE, a phase 3, multicentre, double-blind randomised trial at 66 US sites between March 2023 and June 2025, testing CT-155, an investigational smartphone-based digital therapeutic built on established psychosocial techniques, as an add-on to antipsychotic medication. 457 adults already taking up to two antipsychotics were randomised one to one to the therapeutic or to a digital control app, for 16 weeks. The primary outcome was change in the motivation and pleasure subscale of the Clinical Assessment Interview for Negative Symptoms. Both groups improved: the treatment arm by 6.8 points, the control arm by 4.2, a difference of 2.6 points, confidence interval 4.0 to 1.2, p below 0.001. Retention was high in both arms, 70.4% and 76.5% still active at week 15. Three things are worth holding together. The design is strong - double-blind, a digital control rather than a waiting list, phase 3, large. The effect is modest, a small-to-moderate standardised difference. And the control arm improved substantially on its own, which is what a credible sham app looks like and also a reminder of how much of a digital intervention's apparent benefit is attention and structure. The product is industry-developed and industry-funded, which is normal for a phase 3 trial and still worth stating.
