Trauma

Nearly a third of Ukrainian civilians met criteria for PTSD or complex PTSD during the war

Covered November 6, 2026

By Maxamus Spaulding, BA

A national telephone survey of 2,504 Ukrainian adults found 25.2% met ICD-11 criteria for PTSD and a further 6% for complex PTSD. Complex PTSD was rarer than elsewhere, perhaps because the threat is ongoing.

War produces trauma on a population scale, but measuring it while a war is still being fought is difficult. Few studies have estimated trauma exposure, PTSD and complex PTSD in a sample representative of Ukraine's general adult population since the full-scale Russian invasion in 2022. This study did so through a large telephone survey of 2,504 randomly sampled adults living in regions controlled by the Government of Ukraine. It assessed lifetime traumatic events, war-related stressors, and PTSD and complex PTSD as defined by ICD-11. Respondents reported high levels of both lifetime and war-related trauma. The estimated national prevalence of PTSD was 25.2%, with a further 6% meeting the additional criteria for complex PTSD, a combined prevalence of 31.2%. In models adjusted for age and gender, combined PTSD or complex PTSD was more likely among women (adjusted odds ratio 1.85), people with past military service (1.79), and those with greater cumulative exposure to lifetime traumatic events (1.16) and war stressors (1.21). The rate of complex PTSD was lower than in some other population estimates. The authors suggest this may reflect the different ways traumatic stress can manifest when trauma is continuous, as it is in an active war zone. Several limitations shape how far the figures travel. Status was assessed within a telephone survey rather than through full clinical assessment, so these are survey-based estimates, and the sample covered only government-controlled regions. Still, a combined prevalence near one in three marks the psychological cost of the war for civilians, and the scale of care that will be needed long after it ends.
