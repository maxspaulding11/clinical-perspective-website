Group therapy measurably lowered cortisol

Covered August 14, 2026

60 adults randomized to an 8-week integrative group program — with the body's stress chemistry as the outcome.

Can talk-based therapy leave a measurable fingerprint on your body’s stress chemistry? This randomized trial went looking for one with unusual rigor. Sixty adults were randomly assigned to either an 8-week integrative group program, combining social-emotional skills, slow breathing with heart-rate-variability biofeedback, and cognitive reappraisal, or a dose-matched active control. Rather than relying on a single measurement, researchers profiled the stress hormone cortisol across multiple days from saliva, and also measured cortisol built up in hair, which reflects roughly the prior month. The intervention group showed a clear drop in daily cortisol output compared with control, and that reduction was still present at the 6-month follow-up. Hair cortisol was lower too, backing up the saliva findings, and related stress markers decreased as well. One honest caveat: the trial ran at a single site and was partly commercially funded, so independent replication matters. But the design, multi-day profiling plus hair analysis against an active comparison, makes this a stronger-than-usual signal that structured psychological skills training can register in the body’s stress system, not just in how people say they feel.

The study

Evgeny Pokushalov, et al., PLOS One, July 2026

DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0352095
