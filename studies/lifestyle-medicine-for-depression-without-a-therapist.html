Lifestyle medicine for depression, without a therapist

Covered August 20, 2026

A three-arm trial of an 8-week program delivered by app, by booklet, or not at all.

Can “lifestyle medicine,” the unglamorous basics of activity, diet, sleep, and stress management, actually treat depression when delivered without a therapist? This three-arm trial says yes, at least in the short term. Researchers in Hong Kong randomized 122 adults with probable depression to one of three groups: the same 8-week multicomponent lifestyle program delivered through a smartphone app, the identical program delivered as printed booklets, or a waitlist. The core content was the same across both active arms, psychoeducation, physical activity, nutrition, sleep and stress management, goal-setting, and motivation, just packaged differently. Both worked. At the end of the program, 78% of the app group and 55% of the booklet group had achieved clinically meaningful improvement in depression, versus just under 20% of those left waiting, and the gains held at one- and three-month follow-ups. The app had extra advantages for stress and healthy habits, while the booklet uniquely helped insomnia. The honest caveats: it’s a modest sample, and it was compared with a waitlist rather than against established treatments like therapy or medication. But as a low-cost, scalable option, especially the app, which needs no clinician time, it’s a genuinely encouraging result for reaching people who can’t easily access traditional care.

The study

Cheuk Yin Chow, et al., Journal of Affective Disorders, July 2026

DOI: 10.1016/j.jad.2026.122319
