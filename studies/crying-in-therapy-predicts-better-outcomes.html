Crying in therapy predicts better outcomes

Covered August 5, 2026

Across 58 patients, those who cried more intensely and longer in early sessions reported better results later.

New research shows crying in therapy isn't just emotional release—it may actually predict better outcomes. A study of 58 patients found that when people cried with greater intensity and for longer during early sessions, they reported stronger connections with their therapists and deeper, more meaningful sessions. Therapists noticed it too. Even more striking: patients who cried more intensely and longer showed greater improvement in anxiety and overall mental health symptoms by the end of treatment. What prompts these moments? Mostly therapist interventions encouraging patients to express and explore their feelings—plus interpretations that offered new ways of understanding their experiences. The takeaway: the quality of crying (how deeply and how long) matters more than just whether it happens. Emotional depth in therapy seems to be doing real work.
