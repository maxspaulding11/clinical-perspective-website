  1. Treatment

    CBT cut OCD symptoms sharply -- and changed a brain signal for holding back

    In a randomized trial of 60 adults with OCD, 77.8% of those receiving 12 CBT sessions met response criteria versus 7.7% on the waitlist, and 37% reached remission. The brain's No-Go P3 signal, an electrical marker of inhibition, increased alongside.

    September 25, 2026 · BMC Psychology
  2. Public Health

    States spend more on suicide prevention than they used to. Not where the deaths are.

    Across 306 enacted state budgets for 2021-2026, states explicitly allocated $7.2 billion to suicide prevention and crisis services, rising from $1.05 to $6.55 per person. But 21.6% of state-years allocated nothing, and a state's suicide rate bore no relationship to what it budgeted.

    September 24, 2026 · JAMA Network Open
  3. Personality

    ICD-11 makes clinicians rate personality traits as present or absent. Can they?

    133 Danish clinicians rated 238 patients' ICD-11 personality trait domains both as present/absent and on a 0-4 scale. The yes/no ratings tracked a validated measure moderately well (r = .51 to .57) -- except for Negative Affectivity, where the correlation fell to .27.

    September 23, 2026 · Personality and Mental Health
  4. Diagnosis

    Does semaglutide protect thinking in psychiatric patients? 13,007 records say maybe

    In a retrospective study of 13,007 US patients with psychiatric diagnoses, those starting semaglutide had fewer recorded cognitive problems at 12 months than those on no diabetes drug (mean score 11.1 vs 14.9). But semaglutide was no better than sitagliptin, which undercuts the drug-specific explanation.

    September 22, 2026 · BMJ Mental Health
  5. Trauma

    ICU diaries are meant to prevent PTSD. Correcting for publication bias, they didn't.

    A meta-analysis of 21 studies and 2,180 ICU survivors found that once results were adjusted for publication bias, ICU diaries had no statistically significant effect on PTSD incidence (RR 0.73, CI 0.47-1.12) or severity. Modest benefits for anxiety and depression survived.

    September 21, 2026 · Nursing in Critical Care
  6. Addiction

    In addiction treatment, shame predicted suicidal thinking. Depression was the link.

    Among 265 people in treatment for substance use disorder in Turkiye, self-stigma predicted suicide-related thinking, and of depression, anxiety and stress, only depression carried the effect. Perceived social support weakened the link at every level tested.

    September 20, 2026 · BMC Psychiatry
  7. Treatment

    Finland put 7-session internet CBT for social anxiety into routine care. Did it work?

    Across 1,654 physician-referred patients nationwide, a free 7-session therapist-guided internet CBT programme produced a large improvement in social anxiety (d = 1.09), with 41.1% improving reliably and 1.7% getting reliably worse -- but there was no control group.

    September 19, 2026 · Journal of Medical Internet Research
  8. Personality

    Borderline personality disorder and the body: what 4 weeks of DBT changed

    Patients with BPD paid markedly less attention to internal body signals than controls (MAIA-2 2.10 vs 2.87), and that gap tracked their interpersonal problems. After four weeks of residential DBT, only their self-reported attention improved -- the brain and heartbeat measures did not budge.

    September 18, 2026 · Translational Psychiatry
  9. Myth Check

    Germany prescribes mental-health apps. The evidence behind them is rated "very low."

    A meta-analysis of the eight prescription-eligible depression and anxiety apps in Germany found a moderate benefit for depression (SMD = -0.49) but graded that evidence "very low" quality, and for generalized anxiety the pooled result came from just two trials and was not statistically significant.

    September 17, 2026 · Scientific Reports
  10. Psychosis

    How common is treatment-resistant schizophrenia? Japan's largest study says 28.6%

    Across 205 Japanese hospitals and 18,914 discharged patients, 28.6% of those formally assessed for treatment resistance met criteria -- but only 52.2% were ever assessed, and fewer than half of those identified were prescribed clozapine.

    September 16, 2026 · Neuropsychopharmacology Reports
  11. Trauma

    Two PTSD therapies work. Which works better depends on which studies you keep.

    A meta-analysis of 163 studies and 48,841 people found both Prolonged Exposure and Cognitive Processing Therapy produced large PTSD symptom reductions (Hedges' g = 1.67), but no patient characteristic predicted which therapy worked better until ten unusual studies were removed.

    September 15, 2026 · European Journal of Psychotraumatology
  12. Neuroscience

    Bipolar Disorder May Rewire the Brain's Communication Network

    Scans of 449 people with bipolar disorder and 510 controls across 16 sites found less efficient, more roundabout brain wiring in emotion and reward circuits, with the disruption tracking illness duration and severity.

    September 14, 2026 · Biological Psychiatry
  13. Genetics

    Do more risk genes mean more severe schizophrenia?

    Researchers compared 346 people hospitalized continuously for at least five years with treatment-resistant schizophrenia against more than 18,000 others. Each standard-deviation rise in schizophrenia polygenic score raised the odds of being in that most severe group by 40% to 80%.

    September 14, 2026 · Molecular Psychiatry
  14. Assessment

    A Polish Version of the 'Dark Tetrad' Test Mostly Holds Up, With One Weak Spot

    A 931-person validation of the Polish Short Dark Tetrad found the sadism and psychopathy scales performed well, but the Machiavellianism subscale showed weak internal reliability (alpha = .67).

    September 14, 2026 · Frontiers in Psychiatry
  15. Treatment

    Can a self-paced online program treat PTSD from childhood trauma?

    181 adults with PTSD linked to childhood interpersonal trauma tried an online program combining self-guided modules with optional virtual group sessions. It beat standard care on symptom reduction, with benefits still holding four months later.

    September 13, 2026 · eClinicalMedicine
  16. Neurodevelopmental

    Pesticides in pregnancy and autism traits: a big study finds no link

    Researchers measured organophosphate pesticide breakdown products in the urine of 3,339 pregnant women across 20 US sites and later scored their children's autism-related traits. After full adjustment, prenatal exposure showed no association with those traits.

    September 12, 2026 · JAMA Pediatrics
  17. Myth Check

    Does a parent's depression drag down a kid's grades?

    Researchers tracked about 9,000 families in the Norwegian Mother, Father, and Child Study and compared cousins to strip out shared family background. Small links between parents' psychiatric symptoms and children's 5th-grade test scores disappeared once cousins were compared.

    September 11, 2026 · European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry
  18. Psychopharmacology

    Do antidepressants in pregnancy raise a child's risk of autism or ADHD?

    A meta-analysis pooled 37 studies covering 648,626 antidepressant-exposed pregnancies and 24,967,806 unexposed ones. Raw estimates showed 35% higher ADHD risk and 69% higher autism risk in exposed children, but the associations shrank or disappeared once confounding was accounted for, and fathers' antidepressant use during pregnancy showed a similar pattern.

    September 10, 2026 · The Lancet Psychiatry
  19. Psychedelics

    Can one day of an inhaled psychedelic lift treatment-resistant depression?

    In a phase 2b trial across 16 European sites, 81 adults with treatment-resistant depression received up to three escalating inhaled doses of mebufotenin (GH001) or placebo on a single day. One week later, 57.5% of the drug group (23 of 40) were in remission, compared with 0% of the 41 placebo patients.

    September 9, 2026 · JAMA Psychiatry
  20. Addiction

    Does cannabis actually calm you down?

    In a double-blind trial, 120 regular cannabis users vaped placebo, 20 mg THC, or 40 mg THC. Self-reported stress dropped about equally in all three groups, while heart rate rose significantly more on the high dose than on placebo.

    September 8, 2026 · Neuropsychopharmacology
  21. Stigma

    Do anti-stigma trainings for doctors and nurses actually work?

    Pooling 21 randomized controlled trials, educational anti-stigma programs modestly reduced healthcare workers' negative attitudes toward people with mental illness right after training (SMD = -0.38). Knowledge and willingness to be near patients did not improve, and the attitude gains were not maintained at follow-up.

    September 7, 2026 · Social Psychiatry and Psychiatric Epidemiology
  22. Public Health

    Anxiety among US young adults kept climbing after 2020

    Using nationally representative National Health Interview Survey data on 10,953 US adults aged 18 to 25, researchers compared 2024 with 2020. The odds of reporting an anxiety diagnosis were 31 percent higher, anxiety medication use 32 percent higher, and frequent anxiety 28 percent higher.

    September 6, 2026 · American Journal of Public Health
  23. Digital Health

    Does a mental health app still work without a coach checking in?

    In a three-arm trial recruiting across 13 countries, 825 adults living with chronic medical conditions used a digital wellbeing program either on their own or with weekly phone check-ins. Both versions cut combined anxiety and depression scores by roughly 3 points versus a waitlist, and the two versions were statistically indistinguishable from each other.

    September 5, 2026 · PLOS Medicine
  24. Forensic

    Peer Support After Prison Cut Reconvictions — But a Side Effect Complicates the Picture

    In a Georgia study of 150 people on probation or parole, those who received forensic peer support were reconvicted at a lower rate than matched peers (60.0% vs. 81.3%), though they also tested positive for drugs far more often.

    September 4, 2026 · Psychiatric Services
  25. Diagnosis

    How Often Do Psychiatrists Actually Agree on a Diagnosis?

    September 3, 2026 · Frontiers in Psychiatry
  26. Personality

  28. Psychosis

    A non-dopamine drug target for schizophrenia finally clears a trial

    A four-week phase 2 trial randomly assigned 189 hospitalized adults with acute schizophrenia to one of two doses of the PDE10A inhibitor alofropodect or to placebo. 42.3% of those on the higher dose met the response threshold, compared with 3.8% on placebo.

    August 31, 2026 · CNS Drugs
  29. Genetics

    The first solid genetic map of borderline personality disorder

    The largest genetic study of borderline personality disorder so far compared 12,339 people with BPD to 1,041,717 people without it. It found 11 regions of the genome linked to the disorder, with common variants accounting for about 17.3% of underlying risk.

    August 30, 2026 · Nature Genetics
  30. Neuroscience

    August 29, 2026 · Nature Mental Health
  31. Trauma

    August 28, 2026 · Journal of Anxiety Disorders
